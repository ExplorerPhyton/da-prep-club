Questions scholarship committees actually ask

INTERVIEWS · 5 MIN READ

Collected from members who've sat in front of real scholarship panels. These aren't the only questions you'll get, but they cover most of what comes up.

"Walk me through your application."

Not a request to recite it back. They want to see what you'd choose to lead with if you had thirty seconds. Practice a short, specific answer — not a summary of every activity.

"Why this scholarship, specifically?"

A generic answer here is the fastest way to sound like you're applying to everything. Know what this particular organization actually funds or values, and connect it to something real about you.

"Tell me about a time you failed."

The trap is picking a fake failure — something that's secretly a strength in disguise. Panels have heard "I worked too hard" a thousand times. Pick something that was actually a mistake, and focus more on what you did after than on the mistake itself.

"What will you do with this money?"

Have an actual, specific answer — tuition, a particular program cost, housing — rather than a vague "help with college." Specific answers read as more prepared and more honest.

"What questions do you have for us?"

Always have at least one. A question about how past recipients have used the award, or what the panel looks for that surprises people, tends to land better than a question you could have answered by reading their website.
