Asking for letters of recommendation without the awkwardness

LETTERS · 5 MIN READ

Most of the discomfort around asking for a letter comes from asking too late and too vaguely. Fix both of those and the conversation gets much easier.

Ask at least a month out

Teachers are usually writing letters for a dozen students at once during the same few weeks every fall. Asking a month before your first deadline, rather than two weeks, gets you a better letter and a less stressed recommender.

Ask in person, then follow up in writing

A short in-person or video conversation asking if they'd be willing, followed by an email with the specifics, works better than an email alone. The follow-up email is also where you attach the brag sheet.

Give them something to work with

A generic letter reads as generic because the teacher doesn't have specific material to draw on. The brag sheet template in the resource library asks for: two or three specific moments in their class you remember, what you're applying for and why, and any context about your growth they might not have seen directly. A recommender who has this can write something concrete instead of "hardworking and a pleasure to have in class."

Make the logistics easy

Send deadlines, the submission method (portal upload vs. email vs. mailed form), and a reminder a week before each deadline. The less your recommender has to track, the more likely the letter gets in on time.

Say thank you, twice

Once right after they agree, and again after the letter is submitted, ideally saying what you ended up applying for. It costs nothing and keeps the door open if you need another letter next year.
