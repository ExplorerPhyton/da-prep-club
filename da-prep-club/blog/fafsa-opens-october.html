FAFSA opens October 1 — here's what to have ready

FAFSA · 4 MIN READ

The form itself usually takes under an hour once you're actually filling it in. Almost all of the time people lose comes from stopping mid-form to go find a document. Gather these before October 1 and the whole thing is a single sitting.

What you need

Your Social Security number, your driver's license or state ID if you have one, and your (and your parent's, if you're a dependent student) most recent federal tax return — the FAFSA generally pulls the return from two years prior, so for the 2027–28 form that means 2025 taxes. You'll also want records of any untaxed income, current bank statements, and records of investments if applicable.

Create your account early

Both you and your parent or guardian need your own StudentAid.gov account, created with your own email address — not a shared family email. Do this before the form opens, not the day of, since account verification can take a day or two.

List every school you're considering

Even ones you're not sure about. Adding a school later is more friction than removing one now, and the FAFSA gets sent automatically to every school on your list.

File early, not just on time

Federal deadlines are generous, but a lot of state grants and individual school aid are first-come, first-served out of a limited pool. Filing in the first few weeks it's open is the difference between qualifying for some of that aid and missing it entirely, even if you'd have technically been eligible.
