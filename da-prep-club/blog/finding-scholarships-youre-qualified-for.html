How to find scholarships you're actually qualified for

LISTINGS · 4 MIN READ

Searching "scholarships for high school seniors" gets you thousands of results, most of which you don't qualify for or that have already closed. A better process filters for fit before you spend any time on an essay.

Search by what makes you specific, not generic

Instead of broad searches, search combinations of your intended major, your background, your state, and any specific activity or identity that narrows the field — first-generation, a particular career path, a specific hobby. The awards with the least competition are usually the most specific ones.

Filter by deadline before you filter by amount

A $500 scholarship with a deadline three weeks out is worth more of your time right now than a $10,000 scholarship due in eight months that you'll forget about. Sort by what's actually due soon using the databases in the resource library, then work backward.

Check local before national

National databases are crowded. Local scholarships — through your school, a parent's employer, a community organization, a local business — often get far fewer applicants because they're harder to find, which means better odds. The counselor's office binder and the alumni-funded list in the resource library are good places to start.

Track everything in one place

Once you're applying to more than two or three, memory stops working. Use the application tracker template to log status, deadline, and requirements for each one — it also makes it obvious which essays you can reuse or adapt for a different application.
